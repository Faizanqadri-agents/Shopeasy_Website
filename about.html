Our Story & Mission

Pioneering Life-Saving Connections

BloodConnect was founded to eliminate the agonizing search for emergency blood donors. Here is how our community grew, who leads our mission, and what drives our collective purpose.

Component 1 • Journey

Our Journey & Milestones

A timeline of how community goodwill transitioned into a nationwide digital blood system.

2020
Inception & Emergency Response

Founded by University Volunteers

Initiated by a group of computer science and medical students responding to critical blood supply shortages during regional health emergencies. Started with a spreadsheet and a direct volunteer telephone helpline.

2022
Campus & Community Drives

Mobile Donor Units & University Drives

Expanded across 15 universities and technical campuses, orchestrating quarterly mobile donation drives that registered over 4,000 voluntary donors and collected 2,500 viable whole blood units.

2024
Technological Leap

Digital Matching & Hospital API Integration

Launched the BloodConnect web application, connecting real-time hospital emergency rooms directly with nearby compatible donors. Average emergency match response dropped from 3 hours to under 20 minutes.

2026
Nationwide Scale

25,000 Lives Saved Milestone

Achieved over 25,000 documented transfusions enabled across 48 cities, collaborating with 120+ accredited medical institutions and regional blood banks.

Component 5 • Informational Guidelines

Mission, Core Values & Eligibility FAQ

General overview of our mission and donor screening principles (non-clinical reference).

BloodConnect exists to ensure that no human life is endangered due to a lack of timely, safe blood access. We adhere to pure voluntary non-remunerated blood donation principles, advocating transparent, secure, and equitable access for all community members without cost.
General criteria include being between 18 and 65 years old, weighing at least 50 kg (110 lbs), showing normal vital signs during the pre-donation mini-screening, and having no acute illnesses, colds, or ongoing antibiotic treatments on the donation day.
For whole blood donation, standard medical guidelines require at least 56 days (8 weeks) between donations for adult males, and typically 84 days (12 weeks) for females to allow complete replenishment of red blood cell iron stores.
Personal contact details such as direct telephone numbers and physical residential addresses are never broadcast publicly. Emergency hospital requests are mediated via our verified notification routing system to prevent unwanted spam or solicitations.
